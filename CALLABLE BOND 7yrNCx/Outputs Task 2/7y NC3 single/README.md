| NC | best call year | par coupon (bp) | Δ vs NC2 (bp) | bullet (bp) | spread over bullet (bp) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 2 | 351.11 | 0.00 | 325.19 | **25.92** |
| 3 | 3 | 346.52 | −4.59 | 325.19 | **21.33** |
| 4 | 4 | 340.79 | −10.31 | 325.19 | **15.60** |
| 5 | 5 | 335.37 | −15.74 | 325.19 | **10.18** |
| 6 | 6 | 330.10 | −21.00 | 325.19 | **4.91** |

The bullet (7y par swap rate) is 325.19 bp regardless of NC. A single call at the
earliest permitted year is worth most to the issuer, so the coupon give-up
shrinks monotonically as the non-call period lengthens: ~26 bp at NC2 down to
~5 bp at NC6 (upward-sloping curve).

Effective duration **4.55** (callable) vs **6.15** (bullet); effective convexity
**−150** vs **+46**. `issuer_pnl > 0` = the debt is cheaper to carry = a gain.

Compared with the 7y NC2 **Bermudan** in `../` : a single call at year 3 gives
the issuer less protection (call value 1.3 pts today vs 2.0 pts), so the
downside cushion is smaller and the up-move give-up is smaller too.
